## Outstanding substantive issues
1. **Benchmark amount** ([$1,000] per case-month) and **effective year** ([2030]) are bracketed policy choices.
2. **Missing data.** HHS does not collect case-months with current support owed. The simulations use a proxy: 12 × (OCSE-157 line 2 − line 2e). The OCSE-157 form and 45 CFR 305.2 and 305.31 would need revision.
3. **Redistribution.** States with higher average orders, usually higher-income States, lose share. Example: New Jersey, −63% in the FY2023 simulation. An income adjustment or a phase-in is an open option.
4. **Behavioral risks.** States could lower guideline schedules, enter more $0 orders, or change how they open and close cases. The per-dollar incentive is small (about 1 cent per dollar owed), and the effect on children is unknown.
5. **Interstate counting, and voluntary payments in cases with no order** (line 24), need regulatory definition.
6. **Short title.** "Families First Act" resembles two enacted laws: the Families First Coronavirus Response Act (2020) and the Family First Prevention Services Act (2018), which also amended the Social Security Act. Legislative Counsel may suggest a distinguishing title.
7. **Data quality.** All FY2022–FY2025 figures are preliminary. No CBO estimate exists. Indirect fiscal effects (retained public-assistance collections, means-tested program interactions) are not quantified.
